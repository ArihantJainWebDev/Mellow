# Mellow's character in motion

Creative direction supplied by the owner. These scenes describe the intended experience. Release evidence belongs in the progress and verification records.

Imagine you’re coding, Arihant. Mellow is sitting on the bottom edge of the screen, heels tapping against the bezel. You type for a while. He does his own thing. Then the terminal prints an error and you say, *“Mellow, show me.”* His heels stop. His eyes find the line before his head turns. He looks at the distance, gets up with a little push from both hands, and heads over. When he reaches the error, he plants one hand on the terminal divider, leans out, and puts the other fingertip **beside the exact word** you need to see. He glances back at you. That glance says, *“This one.”*

That’s how I imagine him: **a small character who understands the desktop at the scale of his own body.** A scrollbar is taller than he is. Crossing a maximized window takes effort. Your pointer can travel in an instant; he has little legs. A lot of his personality lives in that difference.

### The desktop is his terrain

- **Windows have weight and edges.** If you slowly drag a window he’s sitting on, he rides along. Yank it quickly and his feet slide, one hand catches the corner, and he has to pull himself back up. Maximize it and he ducks as the ledge rushes toward the top.
- **He finds routes.** To reach a toolbar he might climb the screen edge, step onto a window title bar, then walk across it. If you move the window halfway through, he pauses with one foot raised, looks for his missing step, and chooses another route.
- **He can get comfortable.** He learns that your taskbar comes and goes. At first he braces when it rises. Later he hops aboard without looking. Occasionally he gets the timing wrong and lands sitting down, slightly offended.
- **Gravity gives the tricks a payoff.** He can hang from the top edge by one hand, look down, think better of dropping, and open his tiny umbrella. When he lands, the umbrella takes a moment to collapse. He has to give it a shake.

### His hands can actually do things

- **“Close this window.”** He looks at the window you meant, climbs to its **×**, braces one foot against the frame, and presses with his palm. The real action happens at contact. If a save dialog appears, he releases the button and looks back at you. *Your choice.* For a quick command, he can already be beside the target; you shouldn’t have to wait through a theatrical climb every time.
- **“Which link did you mean?”** He points just outside the link so you can read it. If you scroll, he tracks it briefly. If it disappears, he lowers his hand. No confident pointing at empty space.
- **“Compare these headphones.”** His gaze goes from one battery figure to the other. He holds up one finger for the cheaper pair, then points across to the longer-lasting pair. The little cloud says *“Checking battery life…”* When he has an answer, he turns toward you.
- **“Where did I leave off?”** He opens your project checkpoint, perches beside the unfinished item, and rests a fingertip under the last line you wrote. Memory becomes something he can *show* you.

### He has manners and a life between requests

- When you’re reading, he finds a place in the margin and settles. If a line of text ends up behind him, he notices and shuffles aside. If you drag-select toward his feet, he lifts one foot, then scoots out of the way.
- When a college email changes tomorrow’s quiz time, he catches your eye with one raised hand. If you’re in the middle of typing, he sits on the reminder for a moment. When you pause, he offers it. If you dismiss him, he gives a little nod and gets on with his day.
- When you’re waiting for a build, he might sit beside the progress bar. On a long build he grows bored enough to lie down. When it finishes, he feels the change, pops upright, and checks whether it succeeded before celebrating.
- When you ask for privacy, capture pauses immediately. Then comes the little performance: look away, pivot on one foot, take two steps, cover his eyes. When you invite him back, one eye peeks out first.
- Sometimes he misses a ledge. Sometimes he nails a landing and gives himself a tiny, private fist pump. Most of the time he’s simply good company. The occasional bit of mischief stays funny because he knows how to be quiet.

And over time, **your Mellow should feel a little different from somebody else’s**. Maybe yours usually takes the reckless shortcut down because you keep encouraging him. Maybe he has a favorite corner of your editor. Maybe the two of you develop a ritual where he taps the terminal twice before a demo. Those small, recurring habits could make him feel familiar in a way another hundred poses never would.

The ambition I’d keep in front of us is this: **you should be able to mute Mellow, hide every thought bubble, and still understand what caught his attention, what he’s about to try, and how he feels when it works.** That’s the visual standard I’d build toward.
